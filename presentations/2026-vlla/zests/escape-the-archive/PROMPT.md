# Escape the Archive

## Prompt

"Create a digital escape room for U.S. History where students unlock four locks using clues from primary sources, one of them about the Little Rock Nine. Auto-graded, with hints I can turn off."

## Follow-up answers

The teacher's plain-language answers to /zest-new's questions, and the refinements
asked for while it was built. Everything below goes beyond the one-sentence prompt.

- **Grading:** "Auto-graded, straight into the gradebook. 25 points for each lock
  they open, 100 total."
- **Hints:** "One hint per lock. If they use it, take off 5 points on that lock, and
  tell them that before they tap it. I want to be able to turn hints off."
- **Wrong answers:** "Don't take points off for wrong tries, but I want to see them
  in SpeedGrader, along with which hints they used and how long it took."
- **Save progress:** "Yes. If they close the tab they should come back to the locks
  they already opened."
- **Settings I can change per assignment:** "Hints on or off, and an optional time
  limit in minutes with a countdown they can see. No limit unless I set one. When time
  runs out, submit whatever locks they got open."
- **The four locks:** "Make each one a different kind so it feels like a real escape
  room: a number lock with spinning dials for a year, a word lock with letter wheels,
  one where they pick the right symbol, and one where they put events in order."
- **Sources:** "Real primary sources, short excerpts only, and only quote what you're
  sure is word for word. If you're not sure, summarize it and label it as a summary.
  Use the Gettysburg Address, the Preamble and the 19th Amendment, and for Little Rock
  use President Eisenhower's executive order and his speech from September 24, 1957."
- **Look and feel:** "Make it look like a locked archive room: bookshelves, a
  document case, a door with four padlocks that light up as they open. The documents
  should look like old archive cards with a citation line. Shake the lock on a wrong
  answer, pop it open on a right one, and open the door at the end with their score.
  It has to work on a phone."
